## Blocking findings

### F1 — Repeated answer-form cue and weak alternatives

Section 4.3 requires meaningful alternatives at comparable specificity and says to remove a systematic “longest is correct” signal; it does not impose equal lengths or an option-count threshold. Here the signal comes from the option content, not merely the count: 17 of 18 keyed answers are strictly longer than every distractor, and the same complete-prescription pattern repeats. Keys state the concrete lock order and often a commit/release boundary; distractors frequently propose an openly opposite order, a timeout retry, waiting under locks, or a global lock. The stem already exposes opposite acquisition order in most cases and explicitly says unrelated work must proceed, so those distractors are easy to reject without choosing among plausible coordination designs. For example, i001’s keyed answer is 145 characters against a longest distractor of 84; i012 is 153 against 85. i014 is the sole case where the key is not longest (117 versus 122).

This makes the repeated bank answerable by form and elimination instead of applying the lock/progress invariant. Rework the choice sets so each wrong alternative is a credible, case-specific competing boundary that fails one visible requirement. Preserve the actual correct decision and use concise key wording; do not pad alternatives or target a length/count. The issue applies across all 18 items because the same recipe-versus-short-mistake structure is repeated.

### F2 — Reason omits the selected decision

Section 4.4 requires Reason to identify the decisive condition and decision in one or two concrete sentences. Every current Reason explains why one or two wrong choices fail, but none states the selected coordination action. For example, i002 says reverse pair order can recreate a cycle and changing acquisition order under contention can livelock; it does not say to acquire by stable asset ID before taking either lock. i014 says the callback/switch can deadlock; it does not say to swap the provider configuration under the registry lock and notify after releasing it. Details supplies general mechanism support, but does not repair the omitted decision in the dedicated Reason field. Revise each Reason to state the case’s decisive condition and selected rule, while avoiding a verbatim key paraphrase. Applies to all 18 items.

The repeated details.transfer sentence is generic and identical across the unit, but is nonblocking by itself: the case-specific scenarioApplication and errorCorrection fields do the primary explanatory work. No separate finding is raised for carried source references; the scenario facts themselves support the lock-order conclusions.
