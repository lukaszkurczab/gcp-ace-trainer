# B-02 — Landscape and anti-reference audit

Date: 2026-08-10

## Status and purpose

**Evidence only — no visual direction is selected or approved here.** This
audit turns the brand contract into concrete collision checks before B-03
creates three editable Figma directions. Its purpose is to make a future
Patternly mark recognisable as one product while avoiding the visual shorthand
that would make it look like a cloud vendor, developer tool, certification
issuer, generic AI tutor, or a copied peer.

This is not trademark clearance or legal advice. It is a design constraint for
the Product Owner's later visual review.

## Evidence base

The normative starting point is `docs/06-branding-and-style-direction.md`:
Patternly is calm and evidence-based; its grammar is module, space, connector,
branch, boundary, focus frame and resolved group; a `P` may emerge as a second
reading through structure or negative space. The contract also explicitly
rejects a generic node graph, cloud mark, terminal cliché, developer-tool
hexagon, provider-like treatment and a separate brand per learning family.

The external sources below were inspected as boundary evidence, not copied as
assets or visual direction:

- [Linear brand guidelines](https://linear.app/brand) — compact product
  logomark and restrained monochrome/product presentation.
- [Vercel brand guidelines](https://vercel.com/geist/brands) — a singular
  triangle symbol, used as the short-form brand identifier.
- [GitHub brand toolkit](https://brand.github.com/) — a mature developer-tool
  system with a mascot and broad product/marketing language.
- [AWS architecture icons](https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/icons/) and
  [AWS trademark guidelines](https://aws.amazon.com/trademark-guidelines/) —
  an established cloud-diagram vocabulary and protected provider marks.
- [Microsoft trademark and brand guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/trademarks)
  — protected product symbols, icons and trade dress, including Azure-related
  assets.

Those sources support a conservative conclusion: the closer a proposed mark or
track treatment resembles a well-known provider/developer shorthand, the less
it can carry Patternly's independent learning promise.

## Collision and anti-reference matrix

| Territory to avoid | Why it collides | Required Patternly constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud outline, cloud-plus-check, provider service glyph, or AWS-style architecture tile | Suggests cloud infrastructure ownership or an official certification relationship. | No cloud silhouette, service icon, provider colour system, provider typography, or diagram tile may become the mark, track symbol, badge or achievement. |
| Four-pane square, Microsoft/Azure-like geometry, or a provider-colour mosaic | Risks a false Microsoft/Azure association, especially on the certification track. | Track treatment remains monochrome-capable and subordinate; it cannot use a four-panel square, Microsoft-like colour quadrant, or a provider product icon. |
| Single black triangle or near-equivalent wedge | Reads immediately as a Vercel-like developer-tool identifier. | Do not use a lone filled triangle, upward wedge, or triangle-only app icon. A path/boundary may appear only as part of the distinct Patternly structure. |
| Speed stripe, diagonal orbit, or narrow abstract line mark | Occupies the same compact SaaS/developer-tool territory as established productivity brands. | The `P` must emerge from relationship and negative space, not a single motion stroke or tilted monogram. |
| Mascot, Octocat-like character, robot head, brain, sparkle, or chat bubble | Turns precise practice into generic AI tutoring or developer-community branding. | No character or AI-assistant metaphor. Illustration remains sparse, diagrammatic and instructional. |
| Node graph, blockchain mesh, neural network, or hexagon lattice | The contract already identifies these as generic technical clichés; they obscure the decision/relationship promise. | Connectors are finite and purposeful: one decisive boundary/path must resolve an initially ambiguous group. No decorative webs, gradients of dots, or repeated hexagons. |
| Terminal prompt, brackets, chevrons, code cursor, or command-line frame | Narrows Patternly to a coding tool and falsely represents the certification product. | Monospace remains content notation only. Brand and track marks cannot be terminal symbols. |
| Shield, seal, ribbon, medal, graduation cap, or formal certificate | Implies official accreditation, pass/fail authority, or gamified reward. | Progress remains evidence-led. No mark, app icon, track symbol or session threshold may look like certification or an official result. |
| Trophy, flame, streak counter, confetti burst, or progress-ring mascot | Conflicts with the non-gamified, non-punitive learning promise. | Visual energy comes from a resolved pattern and next useful action, never celebration mechanics. |
| Separate algorithm/cloud product logos or family-specific type systems | Breaks the one-brand architecture and can imply provider affiliation. | One Patternly wordmark and primary mark; only bounded subordinate accents, symbols and motifs may distinguish tracks. |

## Positive design tests for B-03

Each of the three candidate directions must answer all of these before it is
shown for owner choice:

1. At app-icon size, does the mark retain a coherent abstract structure before
   a reader notices the secondary `P` reading?
2. Does the mark express *ambiguous parts → decisive boundary → resolved
   group* without a cloud, node web, hexagon, terminal, mascot, certificate,
   or provider reference?
3. Can the mark, wordmark, and both track symbols work in one colour and in
   Light/Dark without turning semantic state into brand colour?
4. Does the certification track remain recognisably Patternly rather than
   looking endorsed by AWS, Microsoft, Google, or another provider?
5. Can a solo maintainer reproduce the system using a small token set, local
   editable vector sources, an OFL/no-recurring-cost type choice, and a bounded
   illustration grammar?

## B-03 constraints and non-goals

The three directions must be materially different in composition, negative
space, and resolved-path logic — not three recolours of one logo. Each remains
an editable *DRAFT* in Figma. None may use third-party marks, architecture
icons, screenshots, provider colour systems, or downloaded type without a
source/license record. No direction may claim official certification status,
AI tutoring, a guaranteed outcome, or a separate public brand for a family.

This audit does not create an app icon, wordmark, component system, target
screen, asset export, or production code. Those are B-03 through B-05 and the
later design-system handoff.
