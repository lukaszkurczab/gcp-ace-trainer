## Editable candidates

| Figma node | Direction | Distinct visual logic | Included proof surfaces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `10:93` | **A — Boundary Signal** | Dispersed modules become one bounded path; a `P` is a secondary negative-space reading. | App-icon study, wordmark treatment, Coding/GCP accents, first-value hero, available/unavailable practice state and store-tile rule. |
| `10:94` | **B — Resolved Group** | Separate units settle into one readable group; the mark is grouped structure before monogram. | App-icon study, wordmark treatment, Coding/GCP accents, first-value hero, available/unavailable practice state and store-tile rule. |
| `10:95` | **C — Focus Frame** | A frame makes one resolved group and next action legible; the mark is focus/boundary rather than a provider or terminal symbol. | App-icon study, wordmark treatment, Coding/GCP accents, first-value hero, available/unavailable practice state and store-tile rule. |

All three are deliberately different in composition and their interpretation
of the brand grammar. They share only the canonical constraints: one Patternly
brand; subordinate monochrome-capable track symbols; Dark/Light viability; no
provider mark, cloud/service glyph, node mesh, hexagon, terminal cliché,
mascot, certificate, seal, badge, or family sub-brand.

## Decision boundary

X-09A requires the Product Owner to choose **exactly two of A, B and C** for
B-04. The choice should be based on the complete systems — mark, small icon,
wordmark, track relationship and product-state readability — not a single
colour or screen.

Until that choice, no candidate may be translated into production tokens,
assets, components, app icon, Store asset, or target UI. The existing
PKG-04A interaction reference remains the only approved design reference for
its narrow interaction scope.
