inventing a new flow. Each shows:

- one Patternly brand with subordinate Coding Interview and GCP ACE accents;

## A — Boundary Signal

The visual thesis is that dispersed modules become one usable, bounded path.
The cobalt contour carries Coding Interview, amber marks a decisive boundary,
and the optional `P` reading stays secondary to the structure. The system uses
quiet technical contrast rather than provider, terminal or graph symbolism.

Its composition is deliberately asymmetric: a compact left route retains the
node-local sequence; a larger right panel prepares the one bounded session;
feedback forms a seam below it; and the terminal next action crosses the
boundary in a high-contrast light panel. The decision cue is **progress along
a bounded path**, not a centred card with a different colour.

The proof set shows the mark/icon, wordmark, two track accents, Learn Approach
10 primary entry, Guided continuation, authored feedback, terminal next action,
unavailable Weak Review, light/reduced-motion/haptic semantics, and public /
Store hierarchy with an explicit 1024 → 180 → 60 px optical-legibility check.
At the three editable study scales, its distinct reading remains separate
modules, one route and an amber boundary.

## C — Focus Frame

The visual thesis is that a frame makes the useful next action legible. Warm
orange provides a boundary, amber is the resolved focus group, and the frame
is not a cloud, certificate, official-provider mark or terminal symbol.

Its composition is deliberately concentric: one large outer frame suppresses
surrounding choice, then an inner action core makes the primary next action the
only visual destination. Availability language remains at the edge of that
focus space. The decision cue is **intentional narrowing to one action**, not
a route, progress rail or sequence.

The proof set shows the mark/icon, wordmark, two track accents, Focus Practice
10 primary entry, authored feedback, terminal next action, unavailable review,
light/reduced-motion/haptic semantics, and public / Store hierarchy with an
explicit 1024 → 180 → 60 px optical-legibility check.
At the three editable study scales, its distinct reading remains an outer frame
and one resolved focus group.

## X-09B decision boundary

Choose one complete system — not a single colour, icon or screen — based on:

1. distinctiveness without collision or implied provider affiliation;
2. recognisability at small icon scale and as a monochrome mark;
3. legibility of first value, feedback, terminal action and unavailable state;
4. accessible contrast and type hierarchy in Dark and Light;
5. solo-maintainability across app and public/store use.

Until `X-09B`, neither finalist may become repository tokens, assets,
components, app icon, Store material or final target UI.
